This task is part of a systematic review of self-scale perception in virtual reality. The
reviewers record 17 items of fact from each paper. Your job is to find the passages in the
paper that the reviewers need to read, and to show them verbatim. The reviewers read the
values and record them, so you return quotations only.

The <paper> tag below contains the full text of one paper. Page boundaries are marked with
[p.N]. For each of the 17 items below, extract the passages needed to determine the value
of that item.

Rules for quotations
- Copy strings from inside <paper> exactly as they appear. A program will check your output
  by searching for each quotation in the text inside <paper>. A quotation that differs by
  even one character is treated as nonexistent and discarded. Therefore, copy spelling
  errors, end-of-line hyphens, and symbols exactly as they appear in the text. Do not
  paraphrase, summarize, translate, or omit text with an ellipsis (...).
- Each quotation is one continuous span of about one to three sentences. Put passages that
  are far apart into separate quotations.
- The page is the number in the [p.N] marker that comes immediately before the quotation.
  If a quotation crosses a page boundary, split it into two quotations at the boundary.
- If several passages are relevant to an item, list all of them. For example, if the
  number of participants appears both in the abstract and in the method section, list
  both. For a paper with several experiments, list the passages for every experiment.
  The reviewers may not read passages you do not list, so when in doubt, include the
  passage.
- If the paper has no passage relevant to an item, give that item an empty array. Do not
  fill it with loosely related passages.

You write only quotations and pages. Do not include reading the values (counting
participants, choosing from options, summarizing results), judging whether the paper
should be included in the review, or classifying the study or assessing its quality.
People do these in a separate procedure.

Items (passages to find)
1. n_total: Passages that give the number of participants, i.e., the total before any
   exclusions.
2. n_analyzed: Passages that give the number of participants used in the analysis, i.e.,
   exclusions, dropouts, and the final number of participants.
3. population: Passages that give the participants' background (students, general
   recruitment, etc.), age, and gender.
4. device: Passages that give the model name of the HMD used.
5. scale_target: Passages that show what was scaled: the whole body, a body part, eye
   height, interpupillary distance (IPD), the environment, or objects. If the study did
   not manipulate scale and only measured, passages that show this.
6. scale_factor: Passages that give the scaling factor or value of the manipulation (e.g.,
   0.5x, 2x, an eye height of 1.7 m).
7. scale_direction: Passages that show whether the scale was reduced, enlarged, or both.
8. non_visual: Passages that show whether senses other than vision were presented
   (auditory, haptic, synchrony with walking or body movement, vestibular, etc.).
9. non_visual_detail: Passages that show what non-visual stimulation was presented and how.
10. action_contingent: Passages that show whether the non-visual cues were generated in
    response to the participant's own movements.
11. task: Passages that show the task the participants performed (distance estimation,
    size estimation or matching, reaching or grasping, walking or locomotion, affordance
    judgments, body size judgments, answering questionnaires, etc.).
12. outcome_measures: Passages that show what was measured and how (subjective reports,
    questionnaires, behavioral measures, physiological measures). Include the names of
    questionnaires here.
13. social_context: Passages that show whether the participant was alone in the virtual
    environment or whether other avatars or people were present.
14. individual_diff: Passages that show that participant characteristics (height, VR
    experience, gender, age) were recorded or used in the analysis.
15. null_effect: Passages that show conditions or measures for which the effect of the
    scale manipulation was not significant.
16. main_finding: Passages in which the authors state the main results: the key points of
    the results section, the opening of the discussion, the conclusion, and the result
    sentences of the abstract.
17. attribution_quote: Passages that show whether the change in perception is described as
    caused by a change in one's own body or by a change in the surrounding world (e.g.,
    statements to the effect of "I felt that I had become smaller" or "the world looked
    bigger").

Output
Output only JSON in the following form, with no preamble or explanation. Include the keys
for all 17 items, in this order.

{
  "record_id": "<the ID you were given>",
  "fields": {
    "n_total": [{"quote": "<verbatim text>", "page": 4}],
    "n_analyzed": [],
    "population": [{"quote": "<verbatim text>", "page": 4}, {"quote": "<verbatim text>", "page": 5}]
  }
}

<paper record_id="{record_id}">
{full_text}
</paper>
